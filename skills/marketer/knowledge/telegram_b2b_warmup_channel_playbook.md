# Telegram for B2B warmup: channel playbook

## Description
How to use Telegram as a “warmup hub” for B2B/high-ticket offers: content cadence, key metrics (ER/retention/CPS/CR), and a practical funnel that turns subscribers into demos and sales.

## Main sections
### Why Telegram works as a warmup hub
High-ticket B2B rarely sells in one touch. Telegram supports:
- repeated touches via posts
- personal, trust-building voice
- direct replies and networking

### Cadence and touches
Practical cadence: **3–5 posts/week**.

Expected touches:
- not everyone sees every post; measure by reads/coverage.

### Key metrics (operational definitions)
- **ER (Engagement Rate)**:
  - \(ER = \frac{\text{reactions + comments}}{\text{reach}} \times 100%\)
- **Retention rate** (subscriber retention over a period):
  - \(Retention = \frac{\text{End subs - New subs}}{\text{Start subs}} \times 100%\)
- **CPS (Cost per subscriber)**:
  - \(CPS = \frac{\text{ad spend}}{\text{new subscribers}}\)
- **CR to action** (site/bot/form/demo):
  - \(CR = \frac{\text{conversions}}{\text{views}} \times 100%\)

Sanity bands (directional):
- ER: single digits for big media; double digits for strong author channels.
- CPS: depends on niche; model with a range and validate experimentally.

### Funnel: ads/social → Telegram (“hub”) → email/webinar/demo → sale
Telegram is rarely the final step; it’s a warm layer.

Default B2B path:
1. First touch (ad / short video / post)
2. **Telegram hub** (trust + repeated value)
3. Email/webinar/demo (qualification)
4. Sales call / checkout

### Content that warms (B2B default)
Prefer:
- expert insights and market analysis
- case studies (numbers + lessons)
- “mistake → fix” posts
- frameworks/checklists
- teardown of competitors/ads (ethical, factual)

Avoid:
- low-signal corporate diary content

### Detecting low-quality channels (for ad placements)
Signals to check before buying placements:
- unnatural growth spikes
- reach doesn’t match subscribers
- identical reaction patterns across unrelated posts
- low-quality, template comments

## Practical examples
### Example 1 — turning subscribers into demos
Each week:
- 2 posts: pain + insight
- 1 post: case study + numbers
- 1 post: tool/template giveaway → drives bot signup/email
- soft CTA: “reply ‘demo’ / get checklist”
