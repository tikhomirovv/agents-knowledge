# Social algorithms and collaborative filtering (for organic + paid)

## Description
A practical mental model of how social platforms distribute content via collaborative filtering, why early engagement matters disproportionately, and which metrics signal “platform win-win-win”.

## Main sections
### What collaborative filtering means (in practice)
Platforms observe what **many users** watch/click/skip and use that behavior to decide what to show next users.

Practical simplification:
- Early viewers “vote” with their attention.
- The platform uses those votes to choose who sees your content next.

### Audience is segmented (and cross-segment content wins)
Your content is evaluated inside segments (topics/interests). Content that can resonate across multiple adjacent segments tends to scale better.

Action:
- Identify your **primary segment** and 1–2 adjacent segments where your message still fits.

### The platform’s real KPI: session time
For most social platforms:
- More session time ⇒ more ad inventory ⇒ more revenue.

Therefore they prefer content/ads that:
- keep users watching,
- do not “damage” the experience.

### Core metrics that drive distribution
For organic and for paid, the platform uses engagement/attention proxies.

- **CTR**: indicates relevance and curiosity.
- **Watch time / retention** (video): indicates value/engagement.
- **Completion rate**: the strongest simple proxy for “this keeps sessions long”.

Practical rule:
- Improving creative that raises CTR and retention often reduces effective CPM/CPC over time because the platform prefers showing “good” ads/content.

### Relationship types with the platform: aim for win-win-win
Use this lens to audit strategy:

- **Win–Win–Win**: you get customers, platform gets revenue, users get value.
  - Usually sustainable.
- **Lose–Win**: you pay, platform earns, users ignore; you get no results.
  - Most common when creative is weak or targeting is off.
- **Win–Win–Lose**: you exploit loopholes and harm user experience.
  - Typically ends in throttling, disapproval, or bans.

### What to do as a marketer
Checklist:
- Start from the user: what makes them stop scrolling?
- For video, optimize the first seconds (hook) and the overall retention curve.
- For paid social, treat **creative** as the primary lever; targeting is secondary when the message is wrong.

## Practical examples
### Example 1 — diagnosing low delivery
If impressions are low / CPM rising:
- check CTR and watch time first
- then check audience fit / placement fit
- only then tweak bidding mechanics
