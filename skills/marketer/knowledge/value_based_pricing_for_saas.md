# Value-based pricing for SaaS (rule of 1/10)

## Description
How to price a SaaS product based on customer value (not costs), using a simple “1/10 of value” starting rule, a cost floor, and segmentation. Includes a practical pricing calculation workflow.

## Main sections
### Value-based pricing (what it is)
Price should reflect **the economic value created for the customer**, not:
- your development cost,
- competitor price lists.

### The 1/10 rule (starting point)
Start price ≈ **10% of the annual value** delivered.

If value \(\approx \$10,000/year\), a starting annual price \(\approx \$1,000\) creates an obvious ROI for buyers.

As value and trust increase, price can asymptotically move toward a higher share of value (e.g. 15–30%), but start with a clear ROI buffer.

### Step-by-step pricing workflow
1. **Quantify value**
   - cost savings (time, tools, errors)
   - revenue increase (conversion, speed, pricing power)
   - risk reduction (compliance, security)
2. **Compute a cost floor**
   - direct costs: hosting, APIs, payments, support
   - target gross margin (e.g. 70–85% for SaaS)
   - floor price ensures profitability
3. **Apply 1/10 rule**
4. **Segment**
   - by company size
   - by usage intensity
   - by value band
5. **Market adjustment**
   - competitor anchors
   - psychological thresholds
   - go-to-market strategy (penetration vs skimming)

### Segmentation outputs (what to produce)
For each segment define:
- segment definition (e.g., 1–50 employees)
- value estimate (annual)
- price (monthly/annual)
- packaging notes (limits/features)

## Practical examples
### Example 1 — time savings pricing
If your tool saves:
- 8 hours/month
- at \$50/hour
Then annual value ≈ \(8 \times 12 \times 50 = \$4,800\).

Starting annual price by 1/10 rule ≈ \$480/year.

### Example 2 — cost floor check
If direct monthly costs per customer are \$40 and target gross margin is 80%:
- floor price ≈ \(40 / (1 - 0.8) = \$200/month\).

If your 1/10 value price is below \$200/month, you must:
- increase delivered value, or
- change segment, or
- change cost structure.
