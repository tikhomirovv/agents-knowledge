# Traffic sources and go-to-market strategy (early stage)

## Description
How to think about traffic “donors” (sources), why “viral free growth” is usually unreliable, and how to pick an early-stage acquisition strategy without spreading too thin.

## Main sections
### The key concept: a traffic donor
Any acquisition system depends on a **donor** — the place users come from (ads platform, content distribution, search, referrals, app stores, communities).

Practical implication:
- Always name the donor explicitly: “We acquire users from \(X\) via \(Y\) creative into \(Z\) landing/onboarding flow.”

### Virality: not free, and rarely stable
Common “viral” paths:
- **Invites/referrals**: requires strong product incentive and \(K\)-factor \(> 1\).
  - \(K = i \times c\), where:
    - \(i\) = invites sent per user
    - \(c\) = invite conversion rate
- **Platform featuring** (e.g. store editorial): can bring volume, but quality/retention may be poor and the mechanism is not controllable.
- **Social sharing**: works best when the product naturally produces shareable artifacts (filters, face-editing, content creation).

Use virality as:
- a **bonus** and a product feature,
- not your only GTM pillar.

### Paid vs “conditionally free” donors
Two predictable buckets:

1. **Paid donors** (ads):
   - Most controllable and fastest feedback loop.
   - Works when unit economics can be made positive.
2. **Conditionally free donors** (content):
   - “Free” in cash, expensive in time/ops.
   - Compounds over time (library effect), but slower.

Recommendation for most early-stage teams:
- Start with **one paid donor** to get fast learning and predictable volume.
- Build **one content engine** in parallel for compounding long-term.

### Influencers: high variance early
Influencer placements can work, but early-stage risks are common:
- audience quality uncertainty
- attribution difficulty
- format/link friction on some platforms

Safer use:
- once you already have clear positioning and unit economics,
- with repeated collaborations and controlled creative.

### Pick one channel first (anti-spread rule)
Early-stage failure mode: “we run 5 channels badly.”

Channel focus rule:
- Choose **one primary donor**, run weekly creative/targeting iterations until unit economics stabilize.
- Only then add the second donor.

### Practical “first channel” heuristics
These are heuristics, not laws:

- If you need **social demand capture** (people didn’t search yet): start with a social ads donor (e.g. Meta).
- If you have **explicit intent** (people actively search): start with search (Google/Yandex).

## Practical examples
### Example 1 — deciding between paid social vs content
If you need customer conversations and quick learning:
- paid social → landing → signup → manual onboarding
Then build content using the top objections/questions you learn.

### Example 2 — a simple GTM statement
“We acquire US founders via Meta ads using pain-based creatives, drive to a one-page landing, capture email, warm via Telegram/Email, convert via demo.”

## Notes
- Don’t romanticize “organic”. Predictable systems beat legends.
- In early stage, your best marketing asset is fast iteration, not a perfect brand book.
