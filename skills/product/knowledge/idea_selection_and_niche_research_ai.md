# Idea selection and niche research (AI-assisted workflow)

## Description
A practical workflow for finding viable product ideas by choosing a “master niche”, drilling into a subsegment, mining repeatable high-cost problems, and sanity-checking market potential with AI-assisted research.

## Main sections
### Why idea selection is a system, not a lightning bolt
Strong ideas come from:
- structured search
- repeated pattern recognition
- evidence of demand (signals, competitors, spending)

### Step 1 — pick a master niche
Start broad (master segments), then narrow.

Selection heuristics:
- prefer markets with visible spend and steady growth
- choose domains where workflows are repetitive and time-consuming
- don’t limit yourself to “where you worked” (past experience ≠ future demand)

### Step 2 — drill into a subsegment
Pick a concrete job / role / workflow.

Example targeting question:
- “What do they do daily for 2+ hours that is repetitive and painful?”

### Step 3 — mine problems (pain discovery)
Look for:
- tasks that consume large time blocks
- versioning/collaboration pain
- errors caused by messy process
- handoffs and approvals that create delays

Output a “pain list” with:
- pain statement in user language
- frequency (daily/weekly)
- cost proxy (hours × hourly rate / risk)
- current workaround

### Step 4 — assess solution potential
For each pain, propose a few solution shapes:
- workflow tool
- AI extraction/summarization
- template library (micro-product)
- collaboration/versioning layer

Then sanity-check:
- willingness to pay
- plausible price band
- reachable ICP channels

## Practical examples
### Example 1 — “2+ hours/day” problem mining
If paralegals spend most time managing document versions:
- solution shapes: versioned doc platform, AI extraction + templating, standardized templates

- Use structured research + interviews as inputs; AI is a speed multiplier, not a truth oracle.

## Notes
- Competition is often a positive signal. “No competitors” frequently means “no market”.
