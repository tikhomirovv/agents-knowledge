# Product management basics and idea filters

## Description
How to avoid common idea-selection traps and choose product directions with a higher chance of PMF: problem-first thinking, competitors as demand proof, and structured validation.

## Main sections
### Common traps when choosing product ideas
- **Chasing uniqueness**: “no analogs” often means “no demand”.
- **Building without validation**: starting from “we can build it” instead of “someone will pay”.
- **Avoiding competition**: competition is often evidence the market exists.

### A better model: problem → willingness to pay → execution
Start from:
1. A stable, expensive problem
2. A clear group who has it (ICP)
3. Evidence they pay (existing tools, budgets, alternatives)
4. A way to improve an existing solution by a meaningful margin

### Validation methods (high level)
Use a mix:
- competitive analysis (what exists and why people buy)
- customer interviews (B2B)
- traffic tests / landing pages (especially B2C)
- pricing sanity checks (value-based anchors + cost floor)

### Competitors as a signal (how to read it)
Competitors prove:
- demand exists
- a budget line exists

Your job is not “invent a category”, but:
- pick a segment where you can be better (focus, UX, speed, workflow fit)
- avoid copying protected IP; copy patterns and improve execution

## Practical examples
### Example 1 — competitor-based framing
Instead of: “a totally new tool”
Use: “a simpler, more reliable version for a narrower segment”

## Notes
